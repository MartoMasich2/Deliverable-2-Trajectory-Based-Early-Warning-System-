1. Executive Summary & System Overview

The Trajectory-Based Early Warning System represents the operational core of the integrated wildlife monitoring platform, translating real-time GPS data, behavioral analytics, and predictive models into actionable intelligence for conflict prevention, resource management, and community protection. This document specifies the complete operational system, from data collection through field team and community response.

System Vision

An always-on, AI-powered monitoring system that:

  • Analyzes animal movement patterns in real-time to detect trajectories toward human settlements, resource areas, or boundaries
  • Predicts potential conflict scenarios 7-30 days in advance using multi-source data integration
  • Automatically generates contextual alerts with recommended response actions
  • Delivers timely, culturally appropriate warnings to communities at risk
  • Enables field teams to respond decisively within 30-minute windows of vulnerability
  • Provides management with continuous situational awareness and decision support

3. Trajectory-Based Analysis Framework

Conceptual Foundation

Traditional wildlife monitoring systems focus on current location. The Trajectory-Based approach adds critical predictive capability by analyzing movement direction, speed, and consistency to forecast where animals are heading in the next hours-to-days.

Core Trajectory Metrics

1. Heading Vector Analysis

Input: GPS points at 15-minute intervals
Calculation: Determine animal heading (bearing) and speed from last 3-5 location points
Output: Bearing (0-360°), speed (km/h), trajectory confidence (0-100%)

Alert Trigger Examples:
  • Elephant heading consistently toward boundary for 1+ hours = HIGH alert
  • Lion heading toward settlement with increasing speed = CRITICAL alert
  • Erratic heading changes = MEDIUM alert (stress/predator interaction)

2. Geofence Breach Prediction

Input: Current position, heading vector, speed, geofence boundary geometry
Calculation: Project trajectory to determine time-to-breach
Output: Projected breach time, confidence interval, intercept location

Alert Logic:
  • ≤2 hours to breach: CRITICAL alert (dispatch immediately)
  • 2-6 hours to breach: HIGH alert (mobilize teams; community warning)
  • 6-24 hours to breach: MEDIUM alert (monitor closely; standby readiness)
  • >24 hours to breach: No alert (continue monitoring)

3. Proximity & Clustering Analysis

Input: GPS points; Settlements, water points, grazing areas (coordinates)
Calculation: Track animal distance to risk features; Detect clustering (stationary behavior)
Output: Distance to nearest resource; Clustering indicator; Duration at location

Alert Patterns:
  • Animal within 2km of settlement and moving closer: HIGH alert
  • Multiple animals clustering at water point for >4 hours: MEDIUM alert (resource stress)
  • Nocturnal approach to settlement: CRITICAL alert (predator hunting behavior likely)

4. Behavioral Anomaly Scoring

Input: Speed, acceleration, heading consistency, location context (time, season, species)
Calculation: Compare current movement pattern to species-typical baseline for time/season
Output: Anomaly score (0-100); Confidence; Interpretation

Alert Thresholds:
  • Score 80+: CRITICAL anomaly (e.g., elephant running 40+ km/h toward settlement)
  • Score 60-79: HIGH anomaly (unusual behavior worthy of attention)
  • Score 40-59: MEDIUM anomaly (monitor for pattern continuation)
  • Score <40: Normal behavioral variation (no alert)

Multi-Animal Pattern Recognition

System detects patterns across multiple tracked animals simultaneously:

Pattern Indicators Interpretation Alert Response
Herd Coordination Multiple animals moving same heading; Similar speed; Coordinated clustering Family unit or social group moving together (common for elephants) HIGH alert (larger animal group = greater conflict risk)
Predator-Prey Pursuit Carnivore accelerating toward herbivore; Herbivore fleeing in opposite direction; Speed >30 km/h Active predation event or chase sequence MEDIUM alert (monitor for settlement proximity)
Dispersal Event Animals simultaneously leaving core home range; Heading in different directions; Speed >20 km/h Response to external disturbance (drought, human activity, predation) HIGH alert (multiple animals becoming unpredictable)
Aggregation at Hotspot Multiple animals clustering at same location; Increasing numbers over 12-24 hours Resource concentrating animals (water during drought; food availability) MEDIUM alert + resource stress prediction; Community info sharing

Trajectory Integration with Environmental Data

Movement interpretation enhanced by contextual environmental factors:

  • Water Stress Context: If water points are drying, trajectory toward remaining water is expected and escalates concern
  • Seasonal Context: Migration routes vary by season; same trajectory means different risk in dry vs. wet season
  • Historical Pattern Context: Each animal/population has characteristic movement; deviations flagged earlier
  • Time-of-Day Context: Nocturnal approaches to settlements indicate predatory behavior; diurnal movements less concerning
  • Weather Context: Heavy rainfall changes movement; animals seek shelter/high ground
  • Human Activity Context: If military operations, tourism, or veterinary activities detected, erratic movement expected and alerts adjusted

4. Alert Generation & Severity Classification

Alert Classification Hierarchy

CRITICAL ALERTS (Red Zone) - Immediate Dispatch & Community Warning

Definition: Imminent threat to human safety or major livestock losses; Requires immediate response

Triggering Conditions:
  • Animal crossing reserve boundary AND within 2km of human settlement
  • Large carnivore (lion, leopard, wild dog) within 1km of settlement after 18:00 (nocturnal hunting time)
  • Multiple animals (herd) breaching boundary simultaneously
  • Elephant in settlement area or moving toward populated area with speed >20 km/h
  • Active conflict scenario (animal attacking livestock, human confrontation detected)
  • Repeated breach attempts at same location within 24 hours
Required Actions (Target: <10 minutes from alert generation):
  • Automated multi-channel notification: Dashboard ALERT + SMS to dispatch coordinator + Radio alert to all field teams
  • Immediate community notification: SMS/voice to registered contacts in affected area
  • Field team dispatch: Automatic routing to nearest available vehicle(s) with GPS navigation
  • Escalation to coordinator and TAWIRI management

HIGH ALERTS (Orange Zone) - Urgent Response & Preparation

Definition: Significant threat potential; Elevated animal behavior or trajectory toward risk zone

Triggering Conditions:
  • Animal heading toward boundary with consistent trajectory for 1+ hour (ETA <6 hours to breach)
  • Behavioral anomaly score 60-79; Movement speed or pattern highly unusual for species/time
  • Multiple animals clustering at boundary area or adjacent to settlement
  • Large carnivore detected within 5km of settlement (daytime), within 10km (nighttime)
  • Herd-level movement toward boundary (coordinated group behavior)
  • Water point drying with animals concentrating; Conflict risk elevated
Required Actions (Target: 15-20 minutes):
  • Dashboard alert with yellow highlighting; SMS notification to coordinator and supervisory staff
  • Localized community notification to potentially affected areas (precautionary)
  • Field team mobilization: Teams placed on standby; Dispatch prepared but not immediate
  • Escalation to operations center manager

MEDIUM ALERTS (Yellow Zone) - Heightened Monitoring & Standby

Definition: Noteworthy change in animal movement; Warrants close monitoring but not immediate response

Triggering Conditions:
  • Animal accelerating toward boundary (speed increase but still 6-24 hours to breach)
  • Behavioral anomaly score 40-59
  • Single animal within 5km of boundary but not directly heading toward it
  • Nocturnal movement in typical foraging areas (normal behavior but documented)
  • Aggregation at water points in dry season (expected but monitored)
  • Repeated test trajectories toward boundary (animal approaching then retreating)
Required Actions (Target: 30-45 minutes):
  • Dashboard alert with yellow highlight
  • SMS summary notification to coordinator (not voice emergency)
  • Increase monitoring frequency: GPS location checks every 10 min (vs. 15 min standard)
  • Prepare community communication template if escalates

LOW ALERTS (Green Zone) - Monitoring & Logging

Definition: Minor animal movement changes; Routine monitoring and logging

Triggering Conditions:
  • Normal movement within reserve boundaries
  • Expected seasonal migration patterns
  • GPS location anomalies (device glitches, temporary signal loss)
  • Behavioral patterns typical for species/time/season
Required Actions:
  • Dashboard display only (logged for trend analysis)
  • No notification to field teams
  • Automatic documentation in incident database

Alert Customization & Suppression Rules

Scenario Suppression/Modification Rationale
Known Military/Research Operations HIGH and CRITICAL alerts suppressed; Alerts moved to MEDIUM level Human activity causing erratic movement expected; Alerts would be noise
Severe Weather (Heavy Rain, Storm) Anomaly-based alerts suppressed; Trajectory-based alerts enhanced (animals seeking shelter) Aberrant movement expected; Focus on shelter-seeking patterns
Breeding Season (Mating Aggregations) Aggregation alerts suppressed; Trajectory alerts remain active Multi-animal clustering normal; Still monitor for boundary risks
Water Point Drying Events Trajectory-toward-water alerts suppressed; Conflict risk alerts enhanced Movement to water expected; High risk of conflict when multiple animals concentrate
Night Hours (22:00-06:00) All CRITICAL alerts remain; HIGH alerts require additional proximity confirmation Night response slower; Need higher confidence on day alerts
Weekend/Holiday Operations CRITICAL alerts unchanged; HIGH alerts escalated to CRITICAL if human community presence low Reduced staffing; Higher severity assigned to compensate

Alert Escalation Paths

Escalation Example: MEDIUM → HIGH → CRITICAL

14:00 - MEDIUM ALERT

Elephant detected 8km from boundary, heading toward it at moderate speed. System assigns MEDIUM alert; Monitoring hub notified.

14:15 - Heading Confirmation

Next GPS point confirms consistent heading vector toward boundary. Trajectory analysis updates: ETA 45 minutes to breach. Alert escalates to HIGH.

14:30 - Speed Increase

Animal accelerates to 22 km/h. ETA to breach now 20 minutes. Alert escalates to CRITICAL. Immediate dispatch and community notification activated.

14:35 - De-escalation Event

Animal suddenly changes heading 90° (turns away from boundary). Trajectory analysis shows movement back toward reserve interior. Alert downgraded to MEDIUM; Dispatch notified (continue approach but lower priority).

14:50 - All-Clear

Animal 12km from boundary with heading away from all risk areas. Alert downgraded to LOW and logged as "False Alarm - Animal Behavioral Shift." Community alert cancelled; Community messaging sent: "Alert status resolved. Animals have moved away."

Workflow 2: HIGH Alert Monitoring & Conditional Dispatch

Protocol for Upper-Level Alerts Without Immediate Breach

Trigger: Animal heading toward boundary with 2-6 hour ETA to breach

T+0-5 min

Alert Assessment: Hub coordinator reviews alert context, animal history, current field team positions, community status. Determines if immediate dispatch or monitoring-only approach.

T+5-15 min

Team Notification: Field teams placed on standby/elevated readiness. Nearest team advised of situation. Teams review animal profile and expected behavior patterns.

T+15-30 min

Community Advisory (Optional): If HIGH alert sustained for >20 min, precautionary SMS sent to community: "Wildlife in area. Heighten awareness. Keep livestock herded. More updates when confirmed."

T+30 min - Ongoing

Continuous Monitoring: Hub monitors animal trajectory every 10 minutes. Threshold set: If ETA to breach reduces to <2 hours, escalate to CRITICAL and dispatch immediately.

T+60-180 min

Outcome Determination: Either (a) alert escalates to CRITICAL → Dispatch, or (b) animal changes heading and alert downgrades → All-clear notification.

Workflow 3: Predictive Early Warning (7-Day Forecast Alert)

Protocol for Non-Imminent But High-Risk Windows

Trigger: Predictive model identifies 7-day period of elevated conflict risk (e.g., water stress + animal trajectory toward settlement area)

Daily 06:00 (Morning Briefing)

Forecast Summary: Hub manager reviews overnight predictive model output. Summarizes 7-day outlook: "HIGH risk window Days 3-5 due to water stress at Point X + elephant herd positioning."

Daily 07:00 (Community Alert)

Precautionary Community Message: SMS sent to community liaison officers and village leaders: "Forecast shows increased wildlife activity this week in northeast grazing areas. Please advise communities to heighten monitoring."

Daily 07:00-08:00 (Team Briefing)

Field Team Briefing: All patrol teams attend or receive radio briefing on risk outlook. Review expected animal movements, positioning, and pre-positioned response resources.

Days 3-5 (High-Risk Window)

Heightened Operations Mode: Increase monitoring frequency; Reduce alert thresholds (more alerts triggered); Position additional teams strategically; Enhance community communication frequency.

Day 5 (End of High-Risk Window)

Model Update & Outcome Capture: Assess whether predicted risks materialized. Document in model feedback system. Refine model parameters for next cycle.

Workflow 4: Community Reporting & Feedback Loop

Two-Way Community Engagement

Purpose: Enable community to provide ground-truth observations and feedback on system performance

Community Observation

Community member observes wildlife movement, resource condition, or potential conflict situation. Calls or texts community liaison officer with observation.

Report Intake

Liaison officer receives report; Logs into mobile app: location, animal type, description, time. Photo/voice optional. Sends confirmation SMS to community member.

Hub Validation

Hub operator cross-references community report with system data: Does it align with GPS collar locations and model predictions? Flag if discrepancy.

Action Trigger (If Warranted)

If community report indicates ground-truth not reflected in system (e.g., animal actually closer than GPS shows), operator manually escalates alert or dispatches team.

Feedback Closure

Community liaison provides response back to community member: "Thank you for report. Patrol team dispatched. Will follow up."

Learning Capture

After incident closure, feedback from community documented. Model learning system uses this for improvement. Example: "Community spotted animals 3km before GPS collar alert - improve buffer zones."

7. Field Team Interface & Mobile Operations

Mobile App Architecture & Capabilities

Core Mobile App Functions

Real-Time Alerting

PUSH notifications on field tablet/phone when incidents generated. Alert includes animal ID, location, heading, priority. One-tap acknowledgment triggers hub notification.

GPS Navigation

Integrated map showing animal last known location, current heading projection, intercept point, route guidance. Offline maps pre-loaded. Real-time tracking of vehicle position relative to target.

Incident Status Updates

Field team provides status updates: Departed, En Route, On Scene, Engaged, Resolved. Each update timestamped and geotagged. Hub sees real-time team position and status.

Incident Documentation

Photo/video capture of animals, interactions, injuries, damage. Voice notes recording observations. Form submission with structured data fields (animal count, behavior, actions taken, outcome).

Offline Sync

All data saved locally if connectivity lost. Automatic sync when connectivity restored. Queue management ensures data isn't lost in low-signal areas.

Resource Management

Track fuel levels, equipment status, team member availability. Log vehicle maintenance and repairs. Support pre-incident resource planning.

Field Team Workflows

Typical Incident Response Workflow (Field Team Perspective)

T+0 min - Alert Notification

Field team (in vehicle or stationed) receives mobile alert: RED CRITICAL notification "Elephant, 3km NW, heading toward Boundary. ETA 18 minutes." Alert includes map, animal history, recommended approach.

T+1 min - Acknowledgment

Team leader taps "ACKNOWLEDGED" button on mobile app. Hub receives confirmation message with team ID and acknowledgment timestamp. Message sent to team: "Dispatch received. Proceeding to intercept."

T+2-5 min - Departure

Team loads vehicle if not already mobile; Confirms fuel, radios, equipment; Departs toward incident location. Team updates status to "DEPARTED" in app. Hub receives update and begins tracking team vehicle position on map.

T+5-10 min - En Route

Team navigates using app-provided GPS route. Receives updated animal location every 5 minutes from hub (position refresh). If animal changes heading significantly, app alerts team and updates navigation route.

T+12-15 min - Arrival

Team reaches intercept location or identifies animal visually. Updates status to "ON SCENE" in app. Hub receives notification. Operator provides real-time animal location updates based on latest GPS point.

T+15-25 min - Engagement

Team observes animal behavior; Assesses threat level and response strategy. Takes photos/videos as appropriate. Provides voice updates to hub coordinator via radio: "Animal alert but moving away from boundary now. Continuing observation."

T+25-35 min - Resolution

Animal either breaches boundary and team pursues/herds back, or animal changes trajectory and alert resolves. Team updates status to "RESOLVED" in app. Documents outcome in incident form.

T+35-45 min - Documentation & Return

Team completes incident report in mobile app: observations, actions taken, outcome, any issues. Submits photos/videos. Begins return to patrol base or continues routine patrol. Hub closes incident in system with all details captured.

Field Team Safety Protocols

Pre-Incident Safety Checklist

Before departing on any incident response, field team completes safety check:

☐ Vehicle mechanical status (brakes, steering, tires)
☐ Fuel sufficient for round-trip + buffer (never below 1/4 tank)
☐ Communication systems (radio, mobile phone, app)
☐ Safety equipment present: First aid kit, flares, fire extinguisher, spare tire, tools
☐ Team composition adequate (minimum 2 personnel for response; more for dangerous incidents)
☐ Weather conditions safe for travel
☐ Route to incident location verified on map (no obvious hazards)
☐ All personnel have consumed adequate water and food (prevent dehydration/fatigue)

In-Incident Safety Protocols

During active incident response:
• Maintain continuous radio contact with hub (check-in every 5-10 minutes minimum)
• Never approach unknown animals without backup nearby
• If confronted by aggressive animal, retreat immediately; Return fire only if life-threatening situation
• Do not pursue animals on foot (remain in vehicles)
• If vehicle disabled, activate emergency beacon; Wait for backup; Do not abandon vehicle
• If personnel injured, radio hub immediately; Provide coordinates; Medical evacuation can be coordinated
• If encountering human threat (armed poacher, hostile community member), withdraw; Report to hub; Security escalation possible

9. Predictive Models & Forecasting

Core Predictive Models

Model 1: Water Stress Forecast (7-30 Day Outlook)

Input Data Sources: Current rainfall, seasonal forecast (NOAA), soil moisture mapping (satellite), water point surveys (monthly field updates)

Model Type: Random Forest regression + hydrological balance model

Outputs:
  • Probability of water stress in each water point (7-day, 14-day, 30-day horizons)
  • Predicted water point drying sequence (which points dry first)
  • Confidence intervals on predictions
  • Visual heatmap showing water stress zones
Integration with Alerts: Model output feeds into conflict risk model. When water stress high, animal trajectories toward remaining water get escalated alert severity. Community notified of likely resource stress period.

Model 2: Vegetation Availability Forecast (7-30 Day)

Input Data Sources: NDVI satellite imagery (Sentinel-2, weekly), rainfall, temperature, historical phenology patterns

Model Type: NDVI time-series forecasting (Prophet / ARIMA) + machine learning regression

Outputs:
  • Predicted NDVI for each landscape zone (indicating vegetation availability)
  • Likely forage availability in key animal feeding areas
  • Stress periods when animals likely to move seeking food
  • Confidence and anomaly detection
Integration with Alerts: When vegetation predicted to decline significantly, system increases alert sensitivity (lower thresholds) because animals more likely to leave reserve searching for food.

Model 3: Wildlife Movement Prediction (7-Day)

Input Data Sources: Historical GPS movement patterns (2+ years), current animal location, water/vegetation stress, weather

Model Type: Species-specific Markov chain + random walk forecasting with environmental covariates

Outputs:
  • Predicted probability of animal location in each grid cell (7-day horizon)
  • Heatmap showing likely movement zones
  • Predicted boundary crossing probability
  • Forecast updated daily as new GPS data received
Integration with Alerts: Real-time trajectory analysis compares observed movement against predicted movement. Deviations from prediction (animal heading unexpected direction) flagged as anomalies. High-confidence trajectory predictions can pre-position teams before crisis moment.

Model 4: Conflict Risk Composite Score (Daily)

Input Data Sources: Wildlife location, trajectory, water stress, vegetation stress, human activity pattern, historical incident frequency (location), weather

Model Type: Ensemble (gradient boosting + logistic regression) scoring model trained on historical incident data

Outputs:
  • Daily conflict risk score (0-100) for each landscape zone
  • Predicted high-risk windows (7-day forecast)
  • Critical hotspot identification (locations of highest risk)
  • Contributing factor explanation (which factors driving risk up/down)
Model Performance:
  • Target: 75% accuracy at predicting high-risk days
  • False positive rate: <25% acceptable (resource pre-positioning)
  • Model retraining: Monthly with new incident data
  • Validation: Continuous comparison of predictions to actual incidents

Model Feedback Loop & Continuous Learning

How Incidents Improve Models

Incident Occurrence

Real-world incident occurs (conflict, near-miss, false alarm). Captured with full detail in system.

Post-Incident Analysis

Data team reviews incident: Did predictive models anticipate this? If not, why not? What environmental or behavioral signals were missed?

Root Cause Identification

Identified gap could be: Model didn't include relevant variable, threshold was set too high, animal behavior was atypical, data quality issue, or unforeseen external event.

Recommendation & Action

Data team recommends improvement: Add weather variable, reduce alert threshold, add historical data for this animal, improve data collection quality, etc.

Model Update

Model retrained with expanded data or new features. Tested on historical incidents to verify improvement without increasing false positive rate too much.

Deployment

Updated model deployed to production system. Performance monitored in next 30-60 days to confirm improvement.

Model Transparency & Explainability

Hub Operators & Managers need to understand WHY system is alerting:

Example Alert with Explanation:
"HIGH ALERT: Elephant trajectory toward boundary. Time to breach: 3.5 hours. Alert triggered because: (1) Heading vector toward boundary 85% confidence, (2) Water stress score 78/100 (water points drying), (3) Historical data shows 35% of boundary approaches at current time-of-day result in breach, (4) Time-of-day (15:30) is afternoon high-activity period. Combined risk score: 72/100."

This transparency enables operators to:
• Understand confidence level of alert
• Assess whether model reasoning makes sense
• Override alert if they have ground knowledge the model lacks
• Build trust in system over time

10. Data Integration & Real-Time Processing

GPS Data Ingestion Pipeline

Data Flow

1. Collar Transmission: GPS collar (Iridium or Globalstar satellite) captures location every 15 minutes; Transmits to satellite network

2. Cloud Reception: Satellite network operator (vendor) receives transmission; Forwards to cloud API endpoint

3. Data Validation: Automated validation checks: Format valid, coordinates within geographic bounds, timestamp reasonable, no duplicate points

4. Quality Assessment: Accuracy metrics calculated: Horizontal dilution of precision (HDOP), number of satellites, battery level. Low-quality points flagged for manual review

5. Storage: Validated points stored in time-series database (InfluxDB or TimescaleDB) for rapid time-based queries

6. Analysis Trigger: New point triggers trajectory analysis engine; Alerts evaluated; Cache updated with latest position

7. Dashboard Update: Real-time dashboard updated via WebSocket; Map refreshes with new animal position; Basemap updated <1 minute from collar transmission

After-Action Review (AAR) Framework

1. What Happened?
Chronological summary of incident from alert to closure. Focus on facts: when, where, who, what actions taken.

2. Why Did It Happen?
Root cause analysis of trigger event. Was it predicted? Did model anticipate? Environmental/behavioral factors.

3. What Worked Well?
Positive aspects: Fast response, good coordination, effective communication, community cooperation. Recognize achievements.

4. What Could Be Improved?
Gaps or inefficiencies: Delayed alert, team miscommunication, data inaccuracy, system issue. Specific, actionable.

5. Model Feedback
For data team: Did predictive models anticipate this? What variables should be included? Threshold adjustments recommended?

6. System Changes (if applicable)
New alert rule, geofence adjustment, operational procedure change, training gap identified.

7. Follow-Up Actions
Specific tasks assigned with owner and deadline. Tracking to ensure completion.

Offline Alert Capability

Critical Capability: System continues generating CRITICAL alerts even if cloud connectivity lost

How it works:
• Local GPS data cache (last 3 days) stored on hub server
• Geofence definitions pre-loaded and kept current
• Alert rules engine runs locally against cached data
• When new GPS collar data received (via satellite), local processing evaluates alerts
• CRITICAL alerts generated locally and pushed to field team via radio/SMS gateway

Limitations:
• Predictive models cannot run offline (require current weather/satellite data)
• Dashboard visualization unavailable
• Cannot update animal profiles or configure new geofences
• Data syncs to cloud when connectivity restored
